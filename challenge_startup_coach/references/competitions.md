## Diamond Challenge

Run by Horn Entrepreneurship at the University of Delaware. Free, $100,000+ prize pool, 83 teams reached the 2026 finals.

**Timeline (2027 cycle)**

| Date | Stage |
|---|---|
| September 16 | Submission window opens |
| January 14, 5PM EST | Submission deadline |
| February 10 | Advancing teams notified |
| March 9 | Finalists announced |
| April 29–30 | Limitless World Summit |

**Team**

- 2–4 high school students, **aged 14–18 at the submission deadline** — not at registration
- One adult advisor, 21+. Multiple advisors allowed but only one goes on the submission and becomes the sole point of contact
- Each student may join only one team and submit one concept per year
- Contact info and email addresses required for every member and the advisor

**On cross-school teams.** The rules never make the school the participating unit — eligibility is per-student. The site's headline is "Any Idea, Any Team, Any Country," and rule 5 requires participants to *avoid disclosing their school names* unless the school is central to the concept. So cross-school and cross-country teams are clearly contemplated. Note the official rules contain no sentence explicitly granting it; if a student needs certainty, email info@diamondchallenge.org.

**Locked at first submission — both are final**

1. **Track** — Business Innovation (solve a customer problem, generate revenue) or Social Innovation (solve a social problem, impact people or environment)
2. **Pitch format** — live event or virtual/pre-recorded

Requests to change pitch event locations afterward are not accepted. A team that cannot compete in its chosen format **forfeits its place in the pitching round.** For live events, a team member must be physically present.

**Submission materials**

- **Written concept narrative, 3–5 pages** — double-spaced, 12-point font, 1-inch margins, no cover page, English, PDF. Pages over the limit are removed. Should cover: the problem, the customers who have it, how it's solved, why it beats existing alternatives, the underlying economics, the team's collective means, and evidence of traction
- **Introductory video, strictly 60 seconds** — publicly accessible URL. Over-length or unviewable videos are disqualified
- Submissions must clear a **minimum score** to advance. All materials final once submitted

**Pitch round** — 5:00 minutes strict, deck recommended ≤15 slides, then 3:00 minutes of Q&A. Only students who pitched may answer questions. Technical difficulties do not stop the clock.

**Summit** — finalists must attend **in person**; at least one team member present. Visa denial or delay allows a one-year deferment, but failure to attend the following year forfeits the spot.

**Judging** — Business Innovation: perceived feasibility, growth potential, and **wow factor**, equally weighted. Social Innovation adds sustainability and social impact. Wow factor means creativity, ingenuity, persistence, passion, storytelling.

**Prizes** — 1st $12,000, 2nd $8,000, 3rd $4,500 in each track, plus topical prizes (~$1,500) for waste and recycling, technology improving lives, pathways to prosperity, global excellence, and human flourishing.

**Rules that disqualify people**

- Concepts that reached the **final round (Summit)** in a prior year cannot be resubmitted. Concepts that only reached the pitching round *can* return
- Concepts must not have generated more than **$100,000 total revenue** before the deadline
- Non-team contributors who helped conceive the idea must be disclosed by full legal name
- Off-topic content (nudity, illegal, violent) is disqualified at leadership discretion

**Pitch sites** — 23 locations including **Canada: Saskatchewan, Toronto, and Vancouver**, plus Armenia, China (Beijing/Nanjing/Shenzhen), Greece, Guatemala, Haiti, India, Japan, Liberia, Nigeria, Panama, Georgia, Sweden, Thailand, and four US states.

Canada runs a separate site, diamondchallenge.ca, which carries the Canadian event dates. The main site doesn't always mirror it.

## Conrad Challenge

Run with Space Center Houston, presented by Equinor. The technical one — 33 teams reached the 2026 Innovation Summit.

**Timeline (2026–27 cycle)**

| Date | Stage |
|---|---|
| August 27 | Activation Stage opens |
| October 30 | Activation closes / Innovation begins |
| January 8 | Innovation Stage deadline |
| April 21–24 | Innovation Summit, Houston |

**Team** — ages 13–18, 2–5 students plus an adult coach. Teams may span schools, cities, and countries. Fee roughly **$499 per team** from the Innovation Stage onward.

**Categories — pick one.** Aerospace & Aviation, Cyber-Technology & Security, Energy & Environment, Health & Nutrition, The Water Challenge. A separate China Chapter runs in parallel. The technical bar varies sharply between categories; Aerospace entries are genuine engineering, not concept sketches.

**Submissions** — Lean Canvas (12 questions, under 40 words each) → Innovation Brief (10 questions, max 3,000 words) + a 3–5 minute video + a team website.

**Judging** — the Innovation Brief is scored on story, the innovation and its impact, progress to date, market, and business model. Strong teams earn the **Conrad Innovator** title; top scorers become Finalists. At the Summit: an **8-minute Power Pitch plus 20 minutes of Q&A** with a live panel, Shark Tank style, and the EXPO booth is scored separately. Historical rubric weighting: Technical Innovation 25, Technical Practicality 20, business and creativity 55.

**Awards** — Pete Conrad Scholars (one team per category, the top honour), Power Pitch Awards, the Equinor Searching for Better Award, the Donald James Citizenship Award, Glinsky Expo Exhibit Awards, and Space Center Houston Best Entrepreneurial Innovators.

**Coverage note** — Conrad also names ~31 Alternate Finalists who attend if a finalist withdraws; those are announced by name only with no project descriptions. The 2025 record is winners only; Conrad keeps no public archive of past finalists who didn't place.

## Blue Ocean

The lowest barrier of the four, and the right answer for a student with nothing built. Over **23,000 students** competed in 2026, up from 12,700 the year before.

**Timeline** — registration open now; pitch due **February 21, 2027**, midnight local time. The online Blue Ocean Mini-Course must be completed before submitting.

**Team** — ages 14–18, individuals or teams up to 5. Teammates may be from different schools or different countries. Free.

**Submission** — a **five-minute video pitch**. No prototype, no existing business, no school chapter required. Three of this year's regional winners entered entirely alone.

**Judging** — built on Blue Ocean Strategy: find a market nobody is serving, then pursue differentiation *and* low cost simultaneously. A Certificate of Outstanding Achievement goes to the Top 10; the school with the most Top 30 pitches wins $1,000. A **Popular Choice Award** is decided by public vote, so mobilising friends and family genuinely affects one outcome.

**Structure** — four global awards (1st, 2nd, 3rd, People's Choice) plus 1st/2nd/3rd in each of six regions: North America, Latin America, Europe, Africa, Middle East, Asia & Oceania. Regional structure means a student competes against their own region, not the whole field.

**Note** — the official winners page publishes team names, students, schools, and a video link, but no project descriptions.

## DECA

Three separate things run under the DECA name. They get conflated constantly.

### 1. Entrepreneurship written events

**Team** — 1–3 students, all must be DECA chapter members. Qualify through Regionals → Provincials/States → ICDC.

**Six events** — Innovation Plan (EIP), Start-up Business Plan (ESB), Independent Business Plan (EIB), Business Growth Plan (EBG), Franchise Business Plan (EFB), International Business Plan (IBP).

Since 2025–26, **EIP and ESB use a 20-slide pitch deck** rather than a written paper; the others remain 10-page papers. EBG requires the competitor to actually own or operate the business — it cannot be hypothetical.

**Presentation** — 15 minutes including Q&A. Everyone presents and everyone answers. No power or internet supplied, and setup counts against the clock.

**Judging** — the judge role-plays a venture capitalist deciding whether to green-light the concept. **90% content, 10% delivery.** Section order is mandatory and deviating costs points.

**Innovation Plan rubric (100 points)** — Overview 20, Problem 14, Customer Segments 14, Unique Value Proposition 14, Solution 14, Conclusion & Feasibility 14, Professionalism 10. This is the most transparent rubric of any competition here; there is no reason to guess.

**Ontario calendar** — join a chapter in September, registration deadline October 16, **regional qualifying exam November 3–5**, provincials portal opens December 7, **written papers due January 7**, provincials exam January 13–14, provincials late January in Toronto, ICDC late April. Note: students entering written events must *also* write the regional exam. Missing it ends the path regardless of paper quality.

**DECA publishes no winning projects** — the organization protects students' IP, and many take their judged plans to real investors. The rubric is the only map.

### 2. Stock Market Game

Different event, different dates — **frequently mistaken for the entrepreneurship track.**

Teams of 1–3 manage a virtual $100,000 portfolio Sept 8 to Dec 4. Student name submission deadline **October 16**; **asset diversification deadline October 23** (minimum $10,000 each in stocks, mutual funds, and bonds, held through the end — miss it and the team is out regardless of returns). Ranking is **percent return benchmarked against S&P 500 Growth**, so tracking the index won't place. Top 25 teams per region advance to ICDC for a 20-slide deck and 15-minute defense.

Scoring at ICDC: Overview 15, Portfolio Performance 15, Investment Rationale 15, Investment Conclusions 15, **Strategic Reflection 15**, delivery categories 25. Strategic Reflection is worth as much as raw performance — a team that lost money but analyses why rigorously can outscore a lucky one.

### 3. Corporate Challenges

Twelve sponsor-run challenges with **no qualifying rounds** — submit online directly to the sponsor. Must be a DECA member, but nothing else. Cash prizes **$500–$6,000**, and a student can enter as many as they want in one year.

Deadlines spread Oct 30 through Feb 1. The Adobe Food Truck Challenge (Jan 15, first prize $6,000) asks for branding, truck design, menu creation, and social media marketing — no prototype, no data. Each sponsor judges against its own rubric, so **the brief is the rubric**.

## Choosing between them

| Student has | Best fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Test results, prototype, research | Conrad | 45 of 100 points are technical |
| Strong story, real problem, less depth | Diamond | Wow factor ≈ a quarter of the score |
| Only an idea | Blue Ocean | No prototype or team required, free |
| Writing skill, no lab access | DECA written | 90% content, public rubric |
| A DECA membership and no time | DECA Corporate Challenge | One submission, no qualifying rounds |
| Lives in Ontario, no school business club | Blue Ocean or Canada FBLA | DECA requires a chapter; FBLA Canada does not |

## Entering the same idea twice

Underused and entirely legitimate. Deadlines are spread across the calendar and deliverables overlap heavily — a written concept, a deck, a pitch video.

Documented cases from this dataset:
- **Echo/Pulse (VOCL)** — Conrad Pete Conrad Scholar *and* Blue Ocean North America 2nd, same headset, same season
- **WiFind** — Conrad's top Cyber honour in 2025, then a Diamond topical prize in 2026 with the same project
- **retouch** — Diamond finals and Conrad finals in the same season

The one restriction to watch: Diamond bars concepts that reached *its own* final round in a prior year.
